13. Adversarial Analyst

The strongest bear argument is that a favorable defense backdrop is already widely understood while execution remains complex. LHX carries nearly $11B of total debt, has substantial goodwill/intangibles, is reshaping its portfolio, and has just undergone an unexpected CEO transition. The board stated the former CEO's conduct was unrelated to financial reporting, controls, customers or operations, and the company reaffirmed 2026 guidance after Sam Mehta's appointment; that reduces—but does not erase—governance-transition risk. The other key friction is valuation: even after the large drawdown, the FAST Graphs-base scenario produces only about an 8% 2033 IRR. The bull case survives scrutiny because backlog, cash flow and end-market demand are measurable, but the DSM still requires price discipline.
